# Digest: Rudnick (2016), Ocean Research Enabled by Underwater Gliders

**Citation.** Rudnick, D. L. (2016), "Ocean Research Enabled by Underwater Gliders,"
*Annual Review of Marine Science* 8, 519-541, doi:10.1146/annurev-marine-122414-033913.
**Local file.** `context/rudnick-2016-ocean-research-enabled-by-underwater-gliders.pdf`
(private, copyrighted; gitignored).

## What it is

A critical review of the science done with gliders in their first decade of routine
use, organized by scale from basin-scale boundary currents down to turbulence. It sets
out to give skeptics "the background required to form their own opinions" and ends with
a warning about hype. It is the reference for glider operating numbers and for CUGN's
record as of 2015.

## Key facts (page)

- Three research gliders: Spray (Scripps and WHOI), Seaglider (U. Washington), Slocum
  (Webb Research); all buoyancy-driven, satellite-communicating at the surface,
  GPS-positioned, unable to fix position underwater without acoustics (p. 520).
- Operating numbers: to 1000 m, a dive takes ~6 h and covers ~6 km; vertical speed
  0.1 m/s, horizontal ~0.25 m/s (~1 km/h, 0.5 kt); missions 1-6 months; a typical
  4-month mission is 3000 km and ~500 dives; shelf gliders dive more often and have
  shorter missions (p. 521).
- Depth-average velocity from dead reckoning between GPS fixes, accurate to 0.01 m/s
  for a glider in good condition; combined with thermal wind it gives absolutely
  referenced geostrophic velocity, the "fundamental value" of gliders for sustained
  transport observation (pp. 521, 524).
- Deployable from a rigid-hulled inflatable, which is "essential to their fundamental
  economy"; gliders are therefore tied to land and suited to coasts and islands
  (p. 521).
- Cost structure: fixed refurbishment, deployment and recovery costs per mission;
  piloting and communications scale with duration; longer missions cost less per day;
  biofouling is "the ultimate limiter in glider endurance" (pp. 521-522).
- Fleet automation: an extensive control literature exists, but models cannot yet
  predict currents at glider-flight scale, so most research uses human pilots (p. 522).
- Sampling: temporal variability projects onto space; wavelengths shorter than ~30 km
  are contaminated at constant depth but resolved on isopycnals (pp. 522-524).
- CUGN: gliders on CalCOFI lines 90.0, 80.0, 66.7 since 2005, continuously since 2006;
  database of >80,000 profiles, 8,700 glider-days, 182,000 km; 57 times as many
  profiles as CalCOFI stations on line 90.0 in 2010-12 (pp. 524-525).
- CUGN science: physical-biological coupling to fronts; alongshore flow with an
  assimilating model (Todd et al. 2011b); spice sources and mixing (Todd et al. 2012);
  the 2009-10 El Nino attributed to atmospheric teleconnection, not southern advection
  (Todd et al. 2011a); diapycnal mixing (Johnston & Rudnick 2015); productivity with
  satellites (Jacox et al. 2015); the 2014-15 warm anomaly tracked at 50 m on line 90.0
  against the ONI (Fig. 3, p. 526).
- Oregon gliders documented declining coastal dissolved oxygen (Pierce et al. 2012;
  Adams et al. 2013) (p. 526).
- Gliders and models (sec. 7): AOSN was the proving ground; assimilation studied in the
  Mediterranean, U.S. East Coast shelf, Australia, Solomon Sea; gliders "have the most
  value when they ... provide sustained observations with fine horizontal resolution";
  pilot across strong gradients; "in coastal regions, where Argo yields fewer profiles,
  a sustained glider program can be the dominant source of in situ data" (pp. 532-533).
- Reliability: new users find gliders harder than plug-and-play; experienced operators
  perform far better, as the multi-year programs show (p. 533).

## Quotable lines

- "Gliders are not 'better' than established approaches ... but they do provide
  efficient access to certain time and length scales." (p. 533)
- "A fair assessment is that none of these attempts at establishing a sustained
  national or international glider observational program have been successful."
  (p. 534)
- "In my view, a main reason is simply the excess of hype." (p. 534)
- "A good approach would be to back off of claims for the potential of gliders and
  instead focus on doing the best science." (p. 534)

## Differs from canonical

- Generic operating numbers are for 1000 m dives (6 h, 6 km); BOONUS canonical is
  500 m, ~3 h, ~3 km (CUGN practice).
- CUGN totals here are as of 2015; Rudnick et al. (2017) gives later totals. Both are
  acceptable until current totals are computed.

## Relevance to BOONUS

Two uses. First, the authoritative source for glider physics, economics and sampling
characteristics, and for CUGN's record and science as of 2015. Second, the tone
guardrail: its conclusion that prior national glider programs failed through hype is
the reason the BOONUS style rules require leading with demonstrated results and
quantifying every claim. Its section 7 supplies the argument that sustained glider
lines are the dominant in situ constraint on regional models where Argo is sparse.
