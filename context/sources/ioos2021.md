# Digest: Detecting the Coastal Climate Signal: The IOOS Contribution (2021)

**Citation.** IOOS Association, *Detecting the Coastal Climate Signal: The IOOS
Contribution*, July 2021, 11 pp. IOOSassociation.org. Prepared by Josie Quintrell (IOOS
Association) and Margaret Chory (Consortium for Ocean Leadership) with contributions
from, among others, Clarissa Anderson (SCCOOS), Jan Newton (NANOOS) and Dan Rudnick
(Scripps).
**Local file.** `context/IOOS_CoastalClimateSignal_Final.pdf` (private copy; the report
is public).

## What it is

An advocacy report from the association of the 11 IOOS Regional Associations (RAs),
arguing that the coastal manifestation of climate change is regional and needs a
sustained, regionally organized observing system. It presents IOOS's federal-regional
structure, four case studies of climate services, three environmental-justice case
studies, and five funding recommendations.

## Key facts (page)

- IOOS: a national network of regional observing systems linked to 17 federal
  agencies, built over 20 years; all 11 RAs are NOAA-certified so their data carry
  federal-equivalent standing and liability coverage (pp. 1, 3).
- Almost 40% of the U.S. population lives in coastal counties; coastal and ocean
  activities generate 58% of GDP (citing NCA3, 2014) (p. 2).
- U.S. waters span 11 Large Marine Ecosystems from the Arctic to the tropical Pacific,
  plus the Great Lakes (p. 2).
- The climate signal "manifests differently in coastal areas than in the open ocean and
  varies greatly from region to region due to ... upwelling, river input, coastal
  dynamics, geomorphology, weather patterns" (p. 2).
- West Coast case study: SCCOOS, CeNCOOS, NANOOS and AOOS support glider lines that run
  365 days a year with subsurface data inshore and offshore; with satellites and buoys
  they detect marine heat waves; data feed NOAA West Watch (p. 6).
- The 2015 Blob stretched from Alaska to Mexico, drove the largest harmful algal bloom
  on record there, closed the Dungeness crab fishery at a cost above $100M, and caused
  a marine-mammal Unusual Mortality Event (p. 6).
- Hurricane gliders: since 2017 IOOS, NOAA AOML and the U.S. Navy fly gliders into
  hurricane season; data are automatically assimilated into NOAA intensity forecast
  models; four RAs (CariCOOS, GCOOS, SECOORA, MARACOOS) planned 40+ missions in 2021
  (p. 7).
- Gulf of Maine: NERACOOS moorings provide >50% of sustained subsurface and >90% of
  deep-water observations there; documented 2008-2012 warming faster than most of the
  world ocean (p. 5).
- NOAA GOMO (Global Ocean Monitoring and Observing) covers the open ocean at a scale
  "distinct and complementary" to IOOS and supplies boundary conditions for coastal
  models (p. 4).
- Recommendations (pp. 1, 10): (1) expand coastal observations and regional models;
  (2) recapitalize and modernize existing infrastructure including "the national
  network of moorings, profiling gliders, high frequency radar and shore stations";
  (3) invest in new sensors (eDNA) and coupled physical-chemical-biological models;
  (4) expand regional data integration; (5) engage underrepresented communities.
- "There are critical gaps in the nation's coastal observing network, particularly for
  biology" (p. 10).

## Quotable lines

- "A one-size-fits-all approach to coastal observing is not sufficient to meet the
  complexity of communities' needs nor to meet the variable manifestations of climate
  change impacts region to region." (p. 1)
- "IOOS is 'shovel-ready' to address national climate issues where they impact people
  most." (p. 3)
- "An end-to-end observing system must match the temporal and spatial scales of these
  impacts." (p. 10)

## Differs from canonical

- The report treats West Coast glider lines (including CUGN) as IOOS-supported
  infrastructure. Canonical: BOONUS is organized and funded independently of IOOS and
  delivers data to the IOOS Glider DAC and GTS as CUGN does today.
- The report's remedy is to fund IOOS; BOONUS's canonical funding assumption is
  non-federal capital for construction.

## Relevance to BOONUS

The best public statement of the "coastal climate signal" framing BOONUS adopts, with
citable population and GDP figures, the Blob cost figure, and the hurricane-glider
precedent for real-time assimilation. Its recommendation #2 shows that the national
glider fleet is acknowledged to need recapitalization, and its biology-gap sentence
supports the BOONUS biogeochemical sensor case. It also defines the landscape BOONUS
must position itself within: 11 RAs, the Glider DAC, NOAA GOMO, and existing 365-day
West Coast lines.
