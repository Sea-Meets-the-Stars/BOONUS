# Digest: OceanGliders Boundary Ocean Observing Network (BOON) white paper, 2018

**Citation.** Rudnick, D. L., and 31 co-authors (BOON Members), "OceanGliders Boundary
Ocean Observing Network," white paper, revised 26 March 2018, 4 pp. Unpublished
community document.
**Local file.** `context/boon.pdf` (private, gitignored).

## What it is

A four-page case for a global network of regional glider networks observing ocean
boundary currents, written for the OceanGliders program of the Global Ocean Observing
System. It argues that society experiences the ocean through its boundaries, that
boundary currents carry the dominant transports of heat, salt and biogeochemistry, and
that gliders deployed from small boats are the economical way to observe them
year-round. It catalogues existing and planned sustained glider lines worldwide and
lists 32 members from the U.S., Canada, Europe, Japan, Taiwan, South Africa and
Australia.

## Key facts (page)

- Glider operating envelope: profiles to 500-1000 m, 3-6 h per cycle, 3-6 km per cycle,
  ~1 km/h; missions of 3-6 months covering well over 2000 km; GPS and Iridium at the
  surface; deployable from small boats and chartered fishing vessels (p. 1).
- Sensors listed: pressure, temperature, salinity, velocity, turbulent dissipation,
  phytoplankton and zooplankton proxies, dissolved oxygen, CO2, nitrate; pH "as
  sensors mature" (p. 1).
- Glider data are "a natural match for regional models of coastal ocean circulation";
  accurate forecasting depends on initialization at the small scales set by
  topography (p. 1).
- Sustained lines as of 2018: CUGN, three lines for a decade plus a fourth off northern
  California for two years; California Current sections south of the West Wind Drift
  bifurcation 2003-2015; OOI five sections off Oregon and Washington since 2014, one
  Oregon line since 2006; Solomon Sea; western Mediterranean (CNRS 8 yr, SOCIB 6 yr);
  Norwegian Atlantic Current Observatory; Gulf Stream crossings; Ellett Line at 56.5N
  since 2009; OSNAP at 57N since 2015; East Australian Current 2017-19 (pp. 1-2).
- Planned as of 2018: Line P; Canadian AZMP off Halifax; Kuroshio east of Taiwan;
  Agulhas; Florida Current between Key West and north Florida; Canaries/Morocco
  upwelling with RAPID (pp. 2-3).
- BOON's intent: coordination and linkage; regional networks respectful of EEZs;
  year-round sustained observation; near-real-time data to the GTS and CF-compliant
  data to a central data assembly center; improved and sustained QC (p. 3).
- Positioning: complements Argo (connects open ocean to the coast with higher profile
  density and cross-current transects) and OceanSITES (repeated sections connecting
  to moorings); overlaps ship surveys to improve resolution; identifies gaps and fills
  them with the most appropriate technology (p. 3).

## Quotable lines

- "Society experiences the changes in the global ocean through the ocean's boundaries."
  (p. 1)
- "Because boundary currents invariably reside in EEZs, their observation must depend
  on regional efforts respectful of the coastal countries." (p. 3)
- "The ultimate realization will be a global network of regional networks that monitor
  boundary current variability across international borders to the world's benefit."
  (p. 3)

## Differs from canonical

- Framing is boundary-current transport; BOONUS's canonical framing is a coastal
  climate-observing network that uses boundary-current lines.
- Depth 500-1000 m and 3-6 h cycles; BOONUS canonical is 500 m and ~3 h.
- BOON as an active coordinating body; canonical status is dormant to our knowledge.

## Relevance to BOONUS

Source of the name and lineage. Its catalogue of sustained lines is the best single
list of the world's glider observatories as of 2018 and the template for "network of
regional networks". Its Argo/OceanSITES positioning paragraph is reusable almost
verbatim for how BOONUS complements open-ocean systems. Its data-flow model (GTS plus
a central DAC) is what the canonical BOONUS data statement adopts.
